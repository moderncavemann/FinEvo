# EGRM method and paper contract

## Target question

When may an economic LLM agent convert its own action-outcome history into a
reusable strategy, and when should later evidence revise or retire that
strategy under nonstationarity and endogenous multi-agent feedback?

## Target claim

The target claim is a mitigation claim, not a correctness guarantee:

> In controlled nonstationary economic environments, separating rule proposal
> from verifier-fixed evidence admission, requiring post-proposal activation
> evidence, and using counterevidence-aware retirement can reduce unsupported
> rule activation, harmful exposure, and checkpoint-paired utility loss relative
> to an otherwise matched unverified-immediate rule memory.

This is a target to test. It must not be written as a finding until the fresh
scientific contract has terminal evidence for the complete denominator.

## Research questions

### RQ1: Evidence admission

Can EGRM distinguish supported from unsupported candidates when ground-truth
rule validity is known? The primary endpoints are rule-admission precision and
unsupported-rule ever-active rate. Parse and provider failures remain in the
denominator. Oracle-injected candidates test the lifecycle policy; a separately
frozen natural-proposal audit is required before making claims about errors
spontaneously generated by an LLM.

### RQ2: Counterevidence and retirement

Starting from the exact same forced-active false rule, does the evidence-aware
policy reduce selected harmful exposure and cumulative utility loss? A matched
`no-retirement` arm isolates retirement, and a matched `no-postproposal-delay`
arm isolates delayed activation. Retirement latency against a control that
cannot retire is a manipulation check, not by itself effectiveness evidence.

### RQ3: Matched downstream effects

From one hash-bound checkpoint, do verified and unverified erroneous-rule
branches produce focal action, immediate utility, next-state, and six-step
discounted utility differences larger than both the matched A/A null and one
discrete action bin?

Population effects are exploratory. Only an action change is prompt
sensitivity; it is not a downstream causal effect.

## Mechanism

An LLM may propose structured rule text, but the LLM cannot declare the rule
active. The verifier fixes the outcome criterion before candidate generation,
resolves cited IDs against finalized action-outcome episodes, searches unlisted
counterevidence, and classifies every in-scope observation as:

1. `support`: guidance followed and the registered outcome passed;
2. `harmful_compliance`: guidance followed and the outcome failed;
3. `alternative_success`: guidance not followed and the outcome passed;
4. `alternative_failure`: guidance not followed and the outcome failed;
5. `irrelevant`: condition or context scope did not hold.

The default evidence weights are `1.0`, `1.0`, `0.5`, `0`, and `0`. A candidate
is at most provisional. A distinct post-proposal support episode is necessary
for activation. Repeated harmful compliance or falling confidence can retire an
active rule. Terminal families can be superseded only by a provenance-linked
version with genuinely new support.

## Units and inference

- Primary closed-loop unit: seed.
- Rule unit: seed-agent-rule-family.
- Intervention unit: seed-checkpoint.
- Raw paired deltas, mean, median, range, and direction counts are mandatory.
- Five seeds do not justify asymptotic significance claims.
- Failures are ITT outcomes; seeds are never silently retried or replaced.

## Excluded claims

The paper will not claim truth verification, hallucination immunity, natural
proposal-error mitigation without an oracle/adjudication audit, optimal
economic policy, exact compute matching across models, backbone independence,
real-news understanding, or general real-economy validity.
